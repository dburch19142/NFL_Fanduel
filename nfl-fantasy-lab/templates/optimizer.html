When you've uploaded a real FanDuel export, every QB/RB/WR/TE candidate also has to clear this week's matchup rules before the optimizer will consider them: QB must be facing a bottom-10 pass defense (most pass yards allowed/game); RB must be facing a defense that's bottom 10 in both rush yards allowed/game and rush touchdowns allowed/game; WR must see at least 25% of their team's own targets/game and be facing a defense that's bottom 15 in both pass yards allowed/game and pass touchdowns allowed/game; TE must have scored at least one receiving touchdown this season (a proxy for a TD-friendly role) and face that same bottom-15 pass defense; DEF is only eligible when this week's opponent offense is bottom 10 in both interceptions thrown/game and sacks allowed/game — a turnover-prone offense with a poor line sets up easy defensive scoring. Anyone carrying any injury designation — including Questionable — is excluded outright. If too few WR, TE, or DEF clear their rule to fill the roster, that position alone falls back to the full real slate rather than leaving the lineup unbuildable. A lineup also never pairs a team defense with a QB/WR/TE from the team it's playing against that week, since a defensive score comes directly at that offense's expense.

The Top-3 Vegas games strategy instead builds the whole lineup from the three games on the slate with the highest over/under (47+ totals tend to produce big QB scores). QB: the best runner among starters facing a bottom-10 pass defense. WR: the best 25%+ target-share WR on the QB's team, the best WR on the team he's playing, and a team's WR2 from one of the other two games. RB: at least one lead back from a team implied to score 27+, and at least one back at $6,500 or less whose team's lead back is out. TE: only those targeted inside the 10. DEF: an opponent implied to score under about 20, then preferring one facing a backup QB, a line that allows a lot of sacks, and playing at home. The picks behind each rule are listed above the lineup.

The Combined strategy uses every game on the slate. The Top-3 Vegas games rules pick the QB, his WR stack, the opposing WR, both RB rules and the DEF; every other slot comes from players in any game who clear the matchup rules above.

The Alpha Rule uses only the three games with the highest over/under among those kicking off Sunday at 1pm and 4pm ET. QB: the starter on the team implied to score more in the game with the highest over/under. WR: his WR1 and the WR1 of the team he's playing; a WR1 facing a top-10 pass defense gives way to his team's WR2. When the QB's WR1 has under a 22% target share, there's no stack: the bring-back takes the other team's WR1 and its TE. RB1: the fill-in for an injured lead back. RB2 (and RB1 when no lead back is out): a lead back on a team that's favored or implied for 24+, or for one RB slot, an underdog's back with over half his team's carries; preferring one on a team starting a backup QB (it leans on the run) or facing a team whose WR1 is out, then the weaker rush defense. WR3: from the second or third game, never a team starting a backup QB; the WR1 or WR2, whichever role the defense has given up more to, with an 18%+ target share. TE: on a team implied for 23+, with targets inside the 10, ranked by target share and how many points the defense allows TEs. DEF: the one facing the offense implied to score the fewest points, never one facing a player the rules picked. FLEX: the optimizer's pick of the WR2s, top two RBs and TEs from the second and third games. No more than four players come from one game.

The Beta strategy takes its running backs only from teams with a top-10 rushing offense (rushing yards per game) playing a bottom-10 rushing defense (most rushing yards allowed per game) on Sunday at 1pm or 4pm ET; that covers both RB slots and an RB in FLEX. Every other slot comes from players in any game who clear the matchup rules above. If the qualifying teams have fewer than two healthy RBs, the RBs fall back to the matchup rules.

The Echo Rule is the Alpha Rule with the Beta strategy's running backs and seven updates for the cheap picks. Its QB plays in one of the three highest over/under games at 1pm or 4pm ET. Its RBs are the lead backs of teams with a top-10 rushing offense facing a bottom-10 rushing defense on Sunday at 1pm or 4pm ET, from any such game, when they fit under the cap; otherwise the Alpha Rule's RBs stay. It uses only games with an over/under of 46+ (the Alpha Rule's top three if fewer than two qualify) and drops running backs with under 35% of their team's carries unless they're filling in for an injured starter. WR1 is the QB's teammate with a 25%+ target share, WR2 the other team's WR1 with 25%+, and WR3 a team's WR1 with a 15-18% share; every other WR, FLEX included, needs 15%+. A pick under $6,000 projecting below 2.5 points per $1,000 is swapped for a better value, downgrading the priciest pick to make room if needed. FLEX comes from the Alpha Rule's FLEX pool plus the QB's next WR and TE, the other team's next WR, and anyone inheriting an injured starter's role. A FLEX candidate needs a real role (15%+ target share, or 40%+ of the carries for an RB), a good game script (an RB on a favored or 24+ team; no pass catchers on a team favored by 7+) and 2.5+ projected points per $1,000 of salary.

The Charlie Rule is modeled on a winning lineup and uses every game kicking off Sunday at 1pm or 4pm ET. QB: the starter on a favored team in a game with an over/under of 46+ whose top two WRs each draw a 20%+ target share; both WRs go in the lineup (one at FLEX), with the other team's WR1 at 25%+ as the bring-back. RB1: a lead back with half his team's carries and a 20%+ target share on a team implied for 24+. RB2: a workhorse with 75%+ of the carries at $6,000 or less, from any game. WR3: a 20%+ WR at $6,000 or less from RB2's game, the other team's first. TE: $5,000 or less with a target inside the 10 on a team implied for 26+. DEF: $3,500 or less facing a bottom-10 offense in sacks allowed, ranked by its sacks plus two per interception. Picks that would break the cap give way to the next candidate.

The Delta Rule uses the traits three winning lineups shared, from every game kicking off Sunday at 1pm or 4pm ET, and lets the optimizer pick the best-projected lineup that has them all: a starting QB from a game with an over/under of 46+, at least one RB, WR or TE from the team he's playing, only workhorse RBs (70%+ of their team's carries), at least one team WR1 with a 25%+ target share, at least one player from a game under 40, and an underdog defense. No more than four players come from one game.
